# Gluten-Free Easter Koulourakia

Oat-flour version | No ouzo | KitchenAid stand mixer method

## Status

- Status: TEST
- Version: 1.0
- Category: desserts
- Last tested:

## Summary

Gluten-free Easter koulourakia made with a rice flour, sorghum flour, oat flour, tapioca starch, and potato starch blend. This batch worked well with oat flour, but the next test should replace the oat flour with sweet rice flour.

## Yield

About 26-28 cookies.

## Oven

350 F / 175 C

## Bake time

About 17 minutes.

## Chill time

1-2 hours.

## Notes before you start

- Next time, use sweet rice flour instead of oat flour.
- For this batch, oat flour worked well.
- Keep the baking powder at 8 g, not higher.
- Use 1 large orange for the zest, not 2.
- Use room-temperature sheet pans lined with parchment paper.

## Butter temperature

The butter should be softened to about 65-68 F / 18-20 C.

It should dent easily when pressed, but should not look shiny, greasy, or melted.

## Ingredients

### Dry ingredients

- 200 g white rice flour
- 100 g sorghum flour
- 60 g oat flour
  - Next time, use 60 g sweet rice flour instead.
- 60 g tapioca starch
- 35 g potato starch
- 8 g baking powder
- 2 g fine salt
- 3 g xanthan gum

### Wet ingredients

- 125 g unsalted butter, softened to about 65-68 F / 18-20 C
- 20 g light olive oil or neutral oil
- 105 g sugar
- Zest of 1 large orange
- 2 large eggs, room temperature
- 2 egg yolks, room temperature
- 20 g honey
- 1 tsp vanilla
- Up to 1 tbsp milk, room temperature or just slightly warm, only if needed

### Egg wash

- 1 egg yolk
- 1 tsp milk

## Equipment

- KitchenAid stand mixer
- Paddle attachment
- Scale
- 2 baking sheets
- Parchment paper

## Method

### 1. Prep everything

- Line 2 room-temperature baking sheets with parchment paper.
- Fit the KitchenAid with the paddle attachment.
- Make sure the eggs are room temperature.
- Make sure the milk is room temperature or just slightly warm.
- Make sure the butter is softened to 65-68 F / 18-20 C.

### 2. Mix the dry ingredients

In a medium bowl, whisk together:

- White rice flour
- Sorghum flour
- Oat flour
- Tapioca starch
- Potato starch
- Baking powder
- Salt
- Xanthan gum

Whisk very well so the starches are evenly distributed.

### 3. Make the orange sugar

In the KitchenAid bowl, combine:

- Sugar
- Orange zest

Rub the zest into the sugar with your fingers for about 1 minute, until the sugar smells strongly of orange and feels slightly damp.

### 4. Cream the butter, oil, and orange sugar

Add to the mixer bowl:

- Softened butter
- Olive oil

Using the KitchenAid paddle:

- Mix on Speed 2 for about 20-30 seconds to combine.
- Mix on Speed 4 for 2-3 minutes until lighter and creamy.

### 5. Add the eggs and yolks

Add:

- 2 eggs, one at a time
- 2 yolks

Mix on Speed 4, letting each addition blend in before adding the next.

### 6. Add the honey and vanilla

Add:

- Honey
- Vanilla

Mix on Speed 2 just until combined.

### 7. Add the dry ingredients

Add the dry mixture in 2 additions.

Mix on KitchenAid Speed 2 only until the dough comes together.

Do not overmix.

### 8. Adjust with milk only if needed

Check the dough before adding milk.

The dough should be:

- Thick
- Sticky
- Soft
- Holding together

If it already looks like that, do not add milk.

If it feels too stiff or a little dry, add 1 teaspoon milk at a time, up to 1 tablespoon total.

### 9. Chill the dough

Cover the bowl and refrigerate the dough for 1-2 hours.

After chilling, the dough should feel:

- Cool
- Firmer
- Easier to shape
- Still pliable

If it is too firm straight from the fridge, let it sit at room temperature for about 5-10 minutes before shaping.

### 10. Preheat the oven

Preheat the oven to 350 F / 175 C.

Keep the baking sheets:

- Lined with parchment paper
- At room temperature
- Not preheated

### 11. Shape the cookies

Portion the dough into pieces weighing 33-35 g each.

Roll each piece into a rope and shape into twists, loops, or traditional koulourakia.

Keep them a little thicker, not thin. That worked better and helped prevent them from drying out.

### 12. Make the egg wash

Whisk together:

- 1 egg yolk
- 1 tsp milk

Brush the cookies lightly.

### 13. Bake

Bake at 350 F / 175 C for about 17 minutes.

Start checking at about 15 minutes, but this batch baked correctly at 17 minutes.

They are done when:

- Bottoms are lightly golden
- Tops are lightly colored
- They look set
- They are not dark brown

### 14. Cool

Let the cookies cool on the baking sheet for 5-10 minutes, then move them to a rack.

- Chill time: 1-2 hours
- Bake time: About 17 minutes
- Cooling time: 5-10 minutes on the baking sheet, then cool fully on a rack
- Total time:

## Texture targets

- Dough should be thick, sticky, soft, and holding together before chilling.
- After chilling, dough should be cool, firmer, easier to shape, and still pliable.
- Cookies should be shaped a little thicker, not thin.
- Finished cookies should have lightly golden bottoms, lightly colored tops, and a set texture without becoming dark brown.

## Gluten-free / celiac notes

- Confirm all flours, starches, baking powder, xanthan gum, vanilla, and any packaged ingredients are labeled gluten-free or verified safe for celiac needs.
- If using oat flour, use only certified gluten-free oat flour because oats are high risk for gluten cross-contact.
- Sweet rice flour may be a better next-test substitute for oat flour and avoids oat-specific celiac concerns for anyone who does not tolerate oats.
- Use clean parchment and clean sheet pans to avoid cross-contact.

## Low-carb notes

- This is not a low-carb recipe because it uses rice flour, sorghum flour, starches, sugar, and honey.
- Treat as a gluten-free holiday dessert rather than a low-carb staple.
